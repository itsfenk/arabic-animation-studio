# Historical and heritage storytelling

## Research standards

- Prefer primary evidence where it directly answers the question: documents, inscriptions, archaeological reports, contemporary records, manuscripts, official registers, maps, or material culture.
- Use reputable historians and specialist scholarship to interpret evidence and provide context.
- For central or contested claims, compare independent specialist sources and note when they rely on the same earlier source.
- Distinguish contemporary testimony from later chronicles, legends, oral traditions, and modern retellings.
- Record calendar systems, date conversions, place-name changes, boundaries, and transliteration variants.
- Avoid projecting modern national identities, borders, values, or terminology uncritically onto earlier periods.

## Storytelling without fabrication

- Do not invent quotations, conversations, private thoughts, motives, or precise staging for historical people.
- A dramatized reconstruction must be labeled as reconstruction and based on what is known; do not make it look like archival footage.
- When exact clothing, architecture, tools, maps, or landscapes are unknown, use a conservative, period-appropriate visual with a note that the image is illustrative—or use abstract graphics.
- Do not collapse several people or events into one character unless the script explicitly identifies the device as a composite.
- Present competing explanations proportionately to the evidence, not as false balance.

## Heritage and oral tradition

Record who collected the story, where and when it was recorded, language/dialect, community attribution, and variants. Do not claim that one variant is the only authentic version without evidence. Treat living communities respectfully; avoid stereotypes and exoticizing language.

## Visual reconstruction labels

Use clear labels such as `إعادة بناء بصرية توضيحية` or `تصوّر فني مستند إلى الوصف المتاح` when visuals are illustrative. Maintain a visual provenance register for maps, artifacts, photographs, manuscripts, buildings, and generated images.
