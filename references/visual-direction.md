# Visual direction and animation style

## Choose per project

Supported directions may include code-drawn vector/SVG, 2D illustration, cutout animation, 3D animation, motion graphics, illustrated documentary, stop-motion-inspired design, archival/live-action composites, and hybrids. These are creative directions, not a promise that the installed engine can produce each one.

## Visual brief

Record:
- chosen technique and why it serves the story;
- aspect ratio, resolution, frame rate, and safe areas;
- palette, contrast, texture, lighting, and atmosphere;
- typography and Arabic font coverage;
- camera grammar and transition language;
- character/prop/location design;
- motion style and complexity;
- use of diagrams, maps, timelines, labels, and archival material;
- asset sources, licenses, and attribution requirements.

## Visual truthfulness

- Use accurate maps, costumes, architecture, artifacts, tools, and time-period details where factual accuracy matters.
- Label symbolic, representative, or illustrative visuals when viewers may mistake them for documentary evidence.
- Do not generate fake archival photographs, manuscripts, quotations, museum objects, or news footage and present them as authentic.
- Do not put fabricated numerals, labels, inscriptions, or sacred text into generated scenes.
- Check AI-generated text and fine details manually; image generators often corrupt Arabic lettering.
- For religious projects, follow the specific restrictions in `religious-content-policy.md`.

## Hybrid production

A hybrid project may combine 2D/3D animation, vector diagrams, compositing, licensed footage, photographs, and typography. Plan interfaces, color grading, perspective, frame rate, and resolution before production. Keep an asset register. Do not promise a native 3D result from the upstream SVG/Remotion engine; choose a compatible external tool or simplify the treatment honestly.

## Originality

Create original characters and visual identities. Do not reproduce copyrighted characters, creator-specific signature designs, or another channel's recognizable sequence. General cinematography and animation principles may be learned, but the resulting expression must be original.
