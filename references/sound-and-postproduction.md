# Voice, sound design, editing, and mastering

## Voice track

- The voice should be clear, natural, and appropriate to the content; default to Modern Standard Arabic.
- Listen to the actual render, not just a text transcript. Correct pronunciation, prosody, pacing, and breath placement.
- Avoid exaggerated accents unless requested. Do not imitate a living person's voice or imply a generated voice is an actual historical/religious figure.
- Keep factual wording intact during timing edits. Never trim a qualifier that changes the claim.

## Sound design

- Tie sound effects to visible actions and meaningful transitions.
- Use ambience to establish location/time without masking narration.
- Music should support tone and remain below important narration, quotations, and numbers.
- Avoid manipulative or sensational sound design for religious, tragic, or sensitive history.
- Use only original, licensed, public-domain, or properly authorized audio. Record the source and license for each asset.
- Reuse cached sound assets only when their context and license are suitable.

## Mix and technical review

- Review speech intelligibility on speakers/headphones and at low volume.
- Check clipping, distortion, noise, abrupt edits, channel balance, silence, and synchronization.
- Use platform-appropriate loudness/true-peak targets, but measure the actual export and report measurements; do not claim a target was met without measurement.
- Ensure captions are synchronized to the final voice and remain legible.
- Keep stems when available (voice, music, effects) for later editing.

## Cost and credentials

Before using a paid TTS or sound API, estimate or inspect expected usage where possible and get user approval. Never expose credentials. Report credits/cost only when measured from a reliable account/tool response; do not infer from historical examples.
