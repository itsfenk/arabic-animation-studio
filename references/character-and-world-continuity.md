# Character, prop, and world continuity

## Character bible

For each recurring character, document: stable name/ID, role, approved age range if relevant, silhouette, face/hair, proportions, palette, wardrobe by scene/time, props, voice direction, movement vocabulary, and prohibited variations. Use turnarounds or reference sheets where the tool supports them.

## Location and period bible

For each location, record: name/ID, geography, architecture, time period, layout, lighting sources, palette, weather, props, background activity, and continuity constraints. Attach sources to historically significant design decisions.

## Shot-to-shot checks

- Face, hairstyle, height, proportions, costume, accessories, and handedness remain stable unless a change is scripted.
- Props do not teleport, duplicate, switch hands, or change size without an explained action.
- Character position, eyeline, screen direction, and movement direction remain consistent across cuts.
- Lighting direction, time of day, shadows, and environmental details remain coherent.
- Background figures and props must fit the shot crop and perspective.
- A new visual asset must be checked against all established references before being used throughout a sequence.

## Engine implementation

For a code-rig engine, use stable rig parameters and reusable components rather than drawing a new character independently for every shot. For image/video generation, attach the approved reference images and describe the exact aspects that must remain invariant. Record any continuity exception intentionally.
