# Storyboard, shot list, and animatic

## Shot list fields

Each shot should include: shot ID, scene ID, script cue, factual claim IDs if any, duration estimate, shot size, angle, camera movement, location, characters, props, main visible action, lighting, on-screen text, transition, audio cues, and production method.

## Storyboard principles

- One clear visual idea per shot.
- The shot should communicate without requiring an unreadable caption.
- Vary framing and composition when it improves comprehension; do not cut rapidly merely to hit a numeric target.
- Use establishing shots to orient, inserts for evidence/details, close-ups for meaningful reactions, and diagrams when they genuinely clarify.
- Show quantitative or geographic information accurately and legibly.
- Avoid confusing visual metaphors that imply an unsupported causal relationship.
- Plan subtitle-safe areas and Arabic text from the beginning.

## Review before animation

Check every storyboard frame at the expected phone/player size and at full resolution. Verify: subject/action clarity, correct period/context, accurate prop geometry, continuity, composition, visual hierarchy, readable Arabic, source alignment, and no prohibited imagery. Record issues and corrections. For long-form content, review an animatic with rough voice timing before full production.

## Timing

Use actual voice timing after a voice sample or final voice is available. Allow enough screen time for a viewer to read maps, numbers, labels, and Arabic captions. Do not cut during a critical numeral, verse reference, quotation, or key explanatory phrase.
