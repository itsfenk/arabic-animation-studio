# Religious content policy — strict mode

This is the highest-verification mode in the studio. The goal is to avoid attributing false statements or events to God, the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, other prophets, revelation, or Islamic teachings. When certainty is unavailable, restraint is preferable to a dramatic but unsupported detail.

## Core prohibitions

- Never fabricate a Qur'anic verse, hadith, supplication, religious ruling, scholarly quotation, chain of transmission, narrator, source reference, or grading.
- Never paraphrase invented wording as though it were a quotation.
- Never invent a prophet's dialogue, thoughts, motives, emotions, actions, chronology, or unreported circumstances.
- Never visually depict a prophet, including direct portrayal, silhouette, face obscured by light, shadow/person-shaped stand-in, or generated voice/performance intended to represent that prophet.
- Do not use a narrator to impersonate a prophet or present invented first-person testimony as theirs.
- Do not present weak, disputed, fabricated, or unattributed reports as established fact.
- Do not create fictional connective events that change the meaning or sequence of a religious account.
- Do not state consensus (`إجماع`) or a definitive ruling without an appropriate, traceable scholarly source.

## Preferred source hierarchy

Use the most appropriate reliable primary and scholarly sources for the exact subject. Depending on the claim, these may include:

1. The Qur'an: verify exact Arabic wording, surah, verse number, and the chosen trusted mushaf/text edition. Do not use an unverified web quote.
2. Hadith: verify the exact text, collection, book/chapter, hadith number where edition-specific, narrator, and the grading/assessment with a recognized hadith authority. Record whose grading is being cited; different scholars may differ.
3. Tafsir and recognized scholarly works: identify author, title, edition, volume/page or stable digital reference. Distinguish the Qur'anic text from a commentator's explanation.
4. Reliable specialist scholarship and institutional references for context, with the author's method and limitations noted.

A search result or an AI-generated reference list is not a verification source. If the source is a translation, keep the Arabic original where needed and identify the translator.

## Hadith-specific checks

- Check wording against a reliable edition or recognized database that identifies the collection and reference.
- Verify the grading separately and attribute it to the named scholar/source. Do not let the model invent a grade.
- If the wording differs between narrations, record the variant and select only the version supported by the approved source; do not blend variants into a new text.
- If authenticity is disputed or unclear, exclude the narration from affirmative teaching content unless the project is explicitly about the disagreement and the differing scholarly positions are accurately represented.
- Do not infer a hadith number from a quotation or copy a number from an unverified social post.

## Stories of prophets and sacred events

- Narrate only what reliable sources establish.
- Keep commentary, tafsir, later reports, and the Qur'anic/hadith text distinct.
- Do not create dialogue to make a scene more cinematic.
- Use environmental imagery, geography, objects, calligraphy (only verified text), maps, chronology, and neutral explanatory graphics instead of representing a prophet.
- Do not use a generic actor, back view, glowing human shape, or voice as a workaround for the no-depiction rule.
- If the source does not specify a person's appearance, clothing, exact location, or action, do not invent it as historical fact. Abstract visuals may be used if clearly illustrative and not misleading.

## Script and approval gate

Before recording, create a religious claim audit containing: exact claim, category (Qur'an/hadith/tafsir/scholarship/history), exact source, reference locator, verification result, grading attribution where relevant, wording approved for narration, and visual restrictions.

If a critical religious detail cannot be verified:

1. Mark it `UNVERIFIED` or `DISPUTED`.
2. Do not voice or visualize it as fact.
3. Prefer omission or a precise statement that sources differ, if that is relevant and supportable.
4. Ask the user before proceeding only if the unresolved issue changes the project's core message.

A generic disclaimer at the end does not make an unsupported religious claim acceptable. The final reviewer must compare every religious sentence and visual cue against the approved audit.
