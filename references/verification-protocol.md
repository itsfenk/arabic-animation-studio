# Claim verification protocol

## Classify before verifying

For each proposed claim, mark one type:

- `FACT` — directly supported by evidence.
- `INTERPRETATION` — a reasoned reading of evidence, attributed where contested.
- `RECONSTRUCTION` — a plausible scene or detail not directly documented.
- `HYPOTHESIS` — a testable but unresolved explanation.
- `FICTION` — deliberately invented content.
- `UNKNOWN` — evidence is insufficient or inaccessible.

Do not present interpretation, reconstruction, hypothesis, fiction, or unknown material as established fact.

## Claim-level checks

For every claim:

1. Write the claim in a single, testable sentence.
2. Identify the exact source passage, data table, official record, or primary evidence that supports it.
3. Check whether the source actually supports the wording and scope. Watch for exaggeration, missing context, outdated numbers, and causal claims inferred from correlation.
4. Verify names, dates, units, spellings, transliterations, and calculations independently where possible.
5. For central, controversial, quantitative, or reputational claims, seek additional independent high-quality evidence.
6. Record counterevidence and disagreements.
7. Assign a disposition: `VERIFIED`, `QUALIFIED`, `DISPUTED`, `UNVERIFIED`, or `EXCLUDED`.
8. Include only verified claims in unqualified factual narration. Qualified claims must be qualified in the narration or on-screen text. Disputed or unverified claims are omitted unless the video is specifically about the disagreement and presents it fairly.

## Quantitative/scientific claims

Show the equation or method, units, assumptions, source data, conversion steps, and rounding. Recalculate independently. Do not imply precision beyond the source data. State the date/period for changing statistics. Distinguish measured data from estimates and projections. If a calculation relies on a simplified model, say so.

## Attribution and quotations

Never put words in quotation marks unless the exact wording has been checked against a source. Do not invent dialogue for a historical person or attribute a popular saying without credible evidence. Paraphrase where exact wording cannot be confirmed and attribute carefully.

## Gate before script lock

A project cannot pass research approval when a central claim remains `UNVERIFIED` or `DISPUTED` without a deliberate, clearly labeled treatment. Do not let dramatic pacing override this gate. Ask the user for a decision only when omission or qualification changes the central premise.
